Training Treats FAQ

Training treats are one of the easiest products to test honestly because the dog either cares or does not. These answers focus on practical use, not big training promises.

What makes a good dog training treat?

A good dog training treat is small, easy to grab, interesting enough for the dog, and not so messy that you hate using it. For Remy, we care about motivation first. If he does not care about the treat, it is not useful for training, even if the label looks great.

Are freeze dried treats good for training?

Freeze dried treats can be good for training because many dogs find them high value and they are often easy to break into smaller pieces. The downside is that some can crumble in your pocket or leave dust in a treat pouch. That is something we watch for when testing training treats with Remy.

Should dog training treats be soft or crunchy?

Soft treats are usually easier for fast training because the dog can eat them quickly and get back to the session. Crunchy treats can still work, but they may slow things down if your dog needs time to chew. For quick reps with Remy, smaller and faster usually wins.

How small should dog training treats be?

Training treats should usually be small enough that your dog can eat them quickly without stopping the flow of practice. For a Lab, that often means smaller than you think. The reward needs to be worth it, but it does not need to be a giant snack every time.

How many treats is too many during training?

It depends on the dog, the treat size, and the rest of the dog's daily food. The safe move is to use small pieces and keep training sessions short. Remy's Lab does not give diet or medical advice, so ask your vet if your dog has weight, allergy, or health concerns.
