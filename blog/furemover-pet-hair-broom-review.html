What worked

The FURemover rubber broom does something a vacuum can't: the bristles actually grip the carpet fibers and drag hair up from the base of the pile. We're talking hair that's been in there for weeks, packed down by foot traffic. One pass and it's rolling into a clump you can just pick up.

It's also legitimately lightweight. The 58-inch telescopic handle means no hunching, and the whole thing feels like you're holding a standard broom — not a workout machine. And at around $20, it costs less than a single vacuum filter replacement.

Good pick for anyone with a heavy shedder — dog, cat, or apparently a shedding significant other. We tested it on all of the above.

What didn't work

Deep-cleaning thick carpet is a workout. Getting the rubber bristles far enough into heavy pile to pull out the really embedded hair takes real back-and-forth pressure. It's not hard, but don't expect to do the whole house without your arms knowing about it. On hardwood it's effortless — the carpet effort is the trade-off for how well it actually works there.

Remy's take

Remy was mostly unbothered by the FURemover broom — which, for a Lab who treats every object as either a toy or a threat, is basically a glowing endorsement. He did chase it a few times mid-sweep, which made the job take longer but was objectively more entertaining.
